# Working at CCC Intelligent Solutions - Chicago

<br />

After three years of setting up Workday for clients at Accenture, I went back to school. During my Master's at UIUC, I joined CCC Intelligent Solutions in Chicago as a summer intern on their Workday team. I knew the product well, but this time I was seeing it from the other side. The internship was then extended into a co-op that fall and again in spring, so one summer turned into a full year.

At Accenture, my job was to build Workday: business processes, benefit plans, and the rules that decide what happens after a life event or a job change. At CCC, my job was to keep all of that running through the bi-annual releases. Every Workday update is a chance for something that worked yesterday to quietly break. I built 110+ regression scripts for Core HCM in Java, Selenide and Maven, using AI tools like Copilot and Testcase Studio to speed up the work. A testing cycle that took weeks of manual effort became an overnight run, cutting testing time by about 90%. It was my first time reviewing code against a system I had only ever configured, and it changed how I saw it.

I also attended Workday Illuminate in Chicago with my advisor. The theme was AI agents becoming digital coworkers, and one message stuck with me: AI governance cannot be an afterthought. It felt strange and exciting to see where the product was heading right after weeks of testing what already existed.

## <b>My work at CCC</b>

### AI Strategy & Agents

- Researched Workday AI Agents, ASOR and the Agent Marketplace, then built and presented an AI adoption strategy and roadmap to HR technology leadership.
- Prioritized high-impact HR AI use cases, built the business case, and advised leadership on where agentic automation reduces manual effort and where it does not.
- Recommended frontline agents and built an internal use-case demo with Talent and Absence agents.

### Regression Test Automation

- Built 110+ Core HCM regression scripts across Benefits, Absence, Time Off and Talent for release and patch testing, turning weeks of manual testing into an overnight run (about 90% less time).
- Built a custom AI Copilot agent in Testcase Studio to generate and refine the scripts, and ran the suites with Java, Selenide and Maven in lower tenants.
- Designed a regression framework across modules with test-coverage metrics for reporting, and staged test workers and configured Absence leave and PTO plans to support it.

### Workday Finance AI

- Enabled, configured and validated Email Expense, Spend Category and Supplier Invoice recommendations.
- Piloted each feature against process requirements before recommending rollout.

### Reporting & Configuration

- Created 54 calculated fields to organize community group data in a custom report, improving HR data accuracy and usability.
- Built a recruiting pipeline dashboard with Talent Acquisition, plus 3 custom reports and dashboards for HCM operational metrics to support HR leadership.
- Configured Flexible Work Arrangements in a lower tenant to pilot location and hybrid work tracking.

### Production Support & Productivity

- Provided steady-state production support across Core HCM and Benefits in a mature in-house Workday tenant.
- Used ChatGPT, Claude and Microsoft Copilot to speed up documentation and requirements analysis.

Not all of my learning happened at a desk. Some of it came through the internship program itself: networking events, outreach events, fun events, sessions with leadership, and team lunches. Some of it came on the train. Every Tuesday that summer, I took the Amtrak from Champaign to Chicago, leaving at 6 AM and getting back at 11 PM. Coming from a humble background, those rides taught me a lot about people, time, work and cultures. One day I met a vision-impaired UIUC student who was an award-winning accessibility inventor and an international chess medalist. He later showed me his accessible dorm, something I never knew existed. Another day, I talked with a girl from Pakistan about food, movies and culture, which I never imagined happening on a train. From my window seat, I watched cornfields turn into skyscrapers. As a student intern, I moved with everyone else: students boarding at Champaign, daily wage workers and civilians on the train, then the corporate crowd along the river walk. It often made me think about the journey I have taken since childhood to reach this place.

There was not much AI in the work I did at Accenture. CCC gave me clarity on where AI can fill the gaps in that work and where my own judgement still matters. As a consultant, I saw only the functional side: helping clients adopt features, sometimes even selling them, while solving issues inside Workday. Working inside the company showed me the full picture, including how the product roadmap and features are decided alongside everything else the company deals with. Technical skills can be learned. These lessons are what helped me connect the dots, and they will help me, consciously or not, to question, think and decide better as my career grows, with a systems thinking approach.
